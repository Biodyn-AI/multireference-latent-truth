Gene-regulatory-network (GRN) inference from single-cell foundation models is typically benchmarked against a single curated reference, even though available references are incomplete, noisy, and context-biased. We propose a multi-reference latent truth framework that treats each reference database as a noisy evidence channel and estimates per-edge credibility under explicit source dependence. We compare five latent-truth estimators of increasing complexity and find that a hierarchical family-Bayes model achieves the lowest rank disagreement under both source-dropout and family-block-dropout stress tests on a 421,521-edge candidate space. A dual-universe evaluation contrasts a full candidate space with a context-matched universe. The raw precision and F1 gap between the two contexts is large (23-31x), but we show through an explicit decomposition that it is almost entirely a base-rate artifact: the context-matched universe has a 25.8x higher density of evaluable perturbation references, so once metrics are normalized to a denominator-invariant enrichment-over-random the genuine ranking difference between contexts is modest (approximately 1.4x). This reconciles the apparent uplift with universe-aware evaluation and motivates reporting prevalence-normalized enrichment rather than raw precision. We introduce a four-tier dual-universe claim governance policy that restricts promotion to edges supported in both evaluation contexts and show that it reduces overpromotion to 0.2% of the candidate space while achieving denominator-invariant perturbation-backed enrichment of 2.2x (p << 0.05) on pre-registered panels. The promoted set is further validated against a genuinely external ChIP-seq reference (ENCODE, never used in the fusion), where it is enriched 1.77x-2.12x (p < 10^-7), with a clean monotone separation across governance tiers. Leave-one-cohort-out replication across six perturbation cohorts yields a pass rate of 5/6, with the single failure explained by sparse reference support (<10 edges); given the small number of cohorts, we treat this as an exploratory reliability threshold rather than a definitive operating boundary. All analyses run on laptop-scale table-level pipelines, requiring no GPU compute.
